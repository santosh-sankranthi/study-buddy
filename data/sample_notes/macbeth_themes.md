# Macbeth: Key Themes

**Ambition without conscience.** Macbeth's ambition is the engine of the play,
but he repeatedly ignores his own moral misgivings. The witches' prophecy
awakens a desire already present; the play asks whether fate or choice is to
blame.

**Appearance versus reality.** "Fair is foul, and foul is fair." Lady Macbeth
coaches her husband to "look like the innocent flower, but be the serpent under
it." The witches' riddling prophecies sound reassuring but hide destruction.

**Guilt.** After Duncan's murder, Macbeth cannot say "Amen" and hears a voice
cry "Sleep no more." Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking scene shows guilt surfacing
even as she tries to wash imaginary blood away.

**Order and the natural world.** Regicide disturbs nature: horses eat each
other, day turns to night, and the weather is unruly until order is restored.
