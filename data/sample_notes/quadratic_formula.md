# The Quadratic Formula

A quadratic equation has the form a*x^2 + b*x + c = 0, where a is not zero.

The solutions are given by the quadratic formula:

    x = (-b +/- sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a)

The expression **b^2 - 4ac** is the **discriminant** and tells you how many real
solutions exist:

- greater than 0: two distinct real roots
- equal to 0: exactly one real root (a repeated root)
- less than 0: no real roots; two complex conjugate roots

The graph of y = a*x^2 + b*x + c is a parabola. Its vertex (turning point) is at
x = -b / (2a). The roots are where the parabola crosses the x-axis, which is
exactly what the formula finds. Completing the square is an alternative method
that derives the same result.
