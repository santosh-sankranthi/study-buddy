# Solubility Rules

Solubility describes how much of a solute dissolves in a solvent at a given
temperature. "Soluble" salts in water include:

- All group 1 (alkali metal) salts and ammonium (NH4+) salts.
- All nitrates (NO3-), acetates (CH3COO-), and most perchlorates.
- Most chlorides, bromides, and iodides -- except those of Ag+, Pb2+, and Hg2+.
- Most sulfates (SO4^2-) -- except BaSO4, PbSO4, and CaSO4 (slightly soluble).

Compounds that are generally **insoluble**: most carbonates (CO3^2-), phosphates
(PO4^3-), sulfides (S^2-), and hydroxides (OH-) -- except with group 1 or
ammonium. Solubility usually increases with temperature for solids, while gas
solubility decreases as temperature rises. "Like dissolves like": polar solutes
dissolve in polar solvents, nonpolar in nonpolar.
